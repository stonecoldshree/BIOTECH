04 / EXAM SETTER'S INSIDER TIPS
🎯

The 60/100 Advantage

You only answer 60 out of 100 in Part B. Pre-decide your 5 skip-topics (e.g. full bioprocess math). This eliminates panic in the exam hall.

⚖️

Negative Marking Math

In Part B: wrong = −1, right = +3. Your break-even is 25% guess accuracy. Never guess blindly — skip if you're below 40% confident.

🔗

Learn in Clusters

Questions often cross topics. E.g., "Which enzyme in PCR is thermostable and lacks 3'→5' exonuclease?" tests biochemistry + rDNA together. Study mechanisms, not just definitions.

📊

Diagram-First Learning

Draw the lac operon, TCA cycle, antibody structure, and ETC on paper. GAT-B loves questions based on reading diagrams and identifying components.

🔬

Technique Questions = Easy Marks

Questions on PCR, ELISA, FACS, Western blot, and chromatography are direct and predictable. These are 100% scoreable — don't skip them.

⏰

9-Hour Day Structure

Split as: 3 hrs study → 30 min break → 3 hrs study → 30 min break → 2 hrs study → 30 min active recall (close book, write what you remember).

📝

Part A Strategy

Part A (10+2 level) is where you can bank easy marks. Spend the last 2 hours on NCERT Biology + PCM basics. Target 50+/60 in Part A as your safety net.

🧠

Active Recall Over Re-Reading

After every 3-hr block, close your notes and write/speak out everything you remember. This single habit triples retention vs passive re-reading.
